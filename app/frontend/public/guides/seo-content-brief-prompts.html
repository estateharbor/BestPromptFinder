Replace bracketed fields. Run stages in order where noted.

1. Search intent classifier Step 1 
Classify search intent for the keyword below and recommend content format.

Keyword: [PRIMARY KEYWORD]
Optional context: [product/site type, country, language]

Return:
## Intent primary (informational / commercial investigation / transactional / navigational)
## Secondary intents
## Likely SERP features (PAA, video, local, shopping, AI overview, etc.)
## Best page type (guide, comparison, landing page, docs, tool page)
## Reader job-to-be-done (1 sentence)
## Success metric for the page (engagement / rankings proxy / conversion)
## Angles that would FAIL intent

Be decisive. If mixed intent, say which intent should own the H1.
2. SERP gap & entity coverage Step 2 
Act as an SEO strategist. Given the keyword and notes from top results, find gaps.

Keyword: [PRIMARY]
Top-result notes (titles, H2s, unique angles, weaknesses):
[paste SERP notes or summaries]

Produce:
1. ## Shared entities & subtopics (must-cover)
2. ## Gaps nobody covers well (opportunity)
3. ## Differentiator thesis for our page (1–2 sentences)
4. ## Questions from PAA / forums to answer
5. ## Claims that need original data, screenshots, or expert quotes
6. ## Internal link targets we should mention (placeholders OK)

Do not invent ranking stats or traffic numbers.
3. Full SEO content brief Step 3 
Write an SEO content brief for writers/editors.

Primary keyword: [ ]
Secondary keywords: [ ]
Search intent summary: [paste]
SERP gaps / thesis: [paste]
Audience: [ ]
Brand voice: [ ]
Conversion goal: [newsletter / demo / none]

Brief must include:
- Working title + 2 alternates (≤60 characters mindset)
- Meta description draft (150–160 chars)
- H1
- Outline (H2/H3) mapped to intent
- Entity / term checklist
- FAQs (3–5) aimed at SERP features
- EEAT notes (what expertise/experience to show)
- Internal & external link guidance
- Word count range with rationale (not a vanity number)
- “Do not” list (thin sections, keyword stuffing, fake stats)

Output as a paste-ready brief document.
4. Brief → draft section writer Writing 
Write ONLY the section for heading: [H2 TEXT]
Using this brief: [paste relevant brief slice]

Rules:
- Match search intent; lead with the answer in the first 2–3 sentences.
- Use concrete examples; no filler.
- Include [entities/terms] naturally.
- Add one useful list or mini-table if it helps scanning.
- End with a bridge sentence to the next section.
- Do not invent statistics, case studies, or citations.

Length: roughly [N] words.
5. Verify draft vs brief (editor QA) Step 4 
Audit this draft against the brief. Do not rewrite the whole article yet.

Brief: [paste]
Draft: [paste]

Return:
## Intent fit (PASS/FAIL + why)
## Outline coverage checklist
## Missing entities / FAQs
## Thin or fluffy sections
## EEAT gaps
## Title/meta recommendations
## Fix list prioritized (P0/P1/P2)
## Ship verdict: Ready / Needs revision

No fake scores out of 100 unless you define criteria explicitly.
6. Programmatic / cluster brief (topic cluster) Clusters 
Design a topic cluster brief around pillar: [PILLAR TOPIC]

Include:
1. Pillar page angle + intent
2. 8–12 cluster supporting URLs (slug + target query + intent)
3. Which clusters are “build now” vs “later”
4. Internal linking rules (pillar ↔ cluster)
5. Shared FAQs / definitions to keep consistent
6. One differentiator the brand owns across the cluster

Do not invent search volumes. Use qualitative priority: High / Medium / Low based on intent fit and competitive gap notes I provide: [notes].

FAQ

What makes a good SEO content brief prompt?

It should force intent clarity, SERP gap analysis, entity coverage, outline, FAQs, and a verify step—so writers know what to include and editors know how to QA.

Should I put keyword density rules in the brief?

Prefer entity coverage and intent fit over density targets. Density rules often create stuffing; checklists of must-cover terms and questions work better.
