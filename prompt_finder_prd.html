The bet

We are not building another prompt library. That market is saturated and undifferentiated. We are building the layer that sits above every library: the engine that decides which prompt actually solves your problem — and proves it before you run it.

Out of 100,000 prompts, we'll tell you the 3 most likely to solve your problem — and explain why.

The defensible asset is not the prompt collection. Collections get copied. The asset is the ranking data, user-outcome data, model-specific performance data, and the recommendation algorithm that compounds with every search and every "did it work?" vote.

01

The problem & the gap

Every incumbent organizes prompts around broad features — writing, coding, analysis, image — not around the job the person is trying to accomplish. Users repeatedly voice this exact complaint.

So a person with a real task — "analyze this company's quarterly results and tell me if they're bullish or bearish" — is handed a search box and 200 undifferentiated results, no signal on which one fits, whether it's any good, or whether it's ever actually worked.

Three questions go unanswered by today's tools, and each maps to one of our scores:

  • Is this a well-built prompt? — construction quality. Partially solved by SurePrompts.
  • Does it solve my exact job? — task relevance. Solved by no one.
  • Has it actually worked, and on which model? — real-world reliability. Solved by no one; this becomes our moat.

03

Who it's for

Professionals with a job to do and no patience for browsing — they want the right tool, fast, with reasons to trust it.

Primary — The task-driven professional

An analyst, marketer, founder, or operator who thinks in outcomes ("assess these earnings", "launch this campaign"), not in prompt-engineering technique. Values speed, evidence, and being right over exploring a catalogue.

Secondary — The prompt power-user

Builds and reuses prompts, wants versioning, a private library, and compare/customize tools. Becomes a contributor of outcome data.

Later — Teams & B2B

Shared libraries, verified internal prompts, role-based access — the monetization surface once the recommendation engine has earned trust.

06

UX principles

Five commitments that separate a decision engine from a catalogue. Every screen is measured against them.

  • Intent first, categories never. The home screen asks a question, not a menu. "What are you trying to accomplish?" replaces a wall of topics.
  • Show the result, not just the prompt. PromptHero's lesson, generalized: a sample output or benchmark answer beside every recommendation removes the "will this work?" doubt.
  • Always explain the ranking. Every result carries a plain-language "Why this prompt" and — rare in this market — its weakness, before the user commits.
  • Prove before you sell. Score, model compatibility, tested version, sample, alternatives — value is demonstrated, never asserted.
  • Turn browsing into using. One-click customize and compare convert a found prompt into a run, not a copy-paste dead end.

10

Data, provenance & architecture

Trust is a data-integrity problem. Every prompt carries its lineage, and no protected or paywalled content is copied — we index metadata or link to source.

Provenance is a first-class record

Something competitors largely lack. Each prompt tracks: source → license/permission → author → collected → last verified → model tested → version.

The engine reuses what we've already built

The scraping and multi-stage evaluation pipeline already exists in prototype — normalize, junk/low-effort filter, exact + semantic dedup, the quality rubric, purpose classification, library-value, and template extraction. Match, Reliability, and ranking are the new layers on top.

Pipeline — green is prototyped today, blue is net-new for Prompt Finder

Ingest→ Normalize→ Junk filter→ Semantic dedup→ Quality score→ Purpose classify→ Templatize→ Intent parse→ Match score→ Reliability→ Rank & explain

11

The moat — a reliability flywheel

The Reliability score turns every user into a sensor. Each run and each "did it work?" vote makes the ranking sharper — an asset no competitor can scrape.

  • Run telemetry — which model, which version, success/failure, output consistency.
  • Outcome votes — a single "Worked / Didn't work" tap after use, per model.
  • Human verification — periodic re-testing stamps a "last verified" date.
  • Compounding — more usage → better reliability data → better ranking → more usage.

The long-term asset isn't the prompts. It's knowing which prompt works, for whom, on which model.

14

Risks & mitigations

SurePrompts adds match + reliability
Our closest threat could copy the scoring split. Mitigation: race to the outcome-data flywheel — the one thing that can't be copied — and own intent-first UX.
Cold-start on Reliability data
Early scores lack run history. Mitigation: seed with human verification + model tests; show "newly added" honestly rather than faking confidence.
Scraping paid/protected prompts
Legal and trust exposure. Mitigation: open/permitted sources, community submissions, own templates; index metadata or link out for paywalled content.
Intent parsing gets it wrong
Bad parse → bad results. Mitigation: the confirm-and-refine step (Screen 2) keeps the user in control before retrieval.
LLMs write their own prompts
"Why pay/search?" skepticism. Mitigation: we sell proof and speed — tested, ranked, model-matched — not raw prompt text.
Catalogue noise as corpus grows
Discovery degrades at scale. Mitigation: aggressive dedup + the quality gate already prune ~50% of raw scrapes before they're ever shown.
